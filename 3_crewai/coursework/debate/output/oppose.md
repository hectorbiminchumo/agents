I oppose the motion.

AI will **not** replace software engineers, because software engineering is not just typing code. It is a human discipline built on judgment, tradeoffs, communication, and responsibility—things AI can assist with, but not truly replace.

First, engineering is fundamentally about **understanding messy human needs**. Real software is not written from perfect specifications; it is built from vague goals, changing business priorities, legal constraints, and conflicting stakeholder demands. A software engineer translates ambiguity into a working system. AI can generate code, but it cannot own the problem, clarify the requirements, or negotiate the tradeoffs when the goal itself is unclear.

Second, software engineering requires **accountability**. When a system fails, leaks data, crashes, or harms users, someone must be responsible. Companies, governments, and clients will not accept “the AI did it” as an answer. Human engineers are the ones who make decisions, sign off on risk, and stand behind the product. That responsibility is not optional; it is central to the job.

Third, AI is still **fragile and unreliable**. It can produce convincing code, but not consistently correct architecture, secure systems, or maintainable long-term solutions. It often lacks true understanding of context and can confidently make subtle mistakes. In engineering, one hidden bug or security flaw can cost millions. That is why AI will remain a tool in the hands of engineers, not a replacement for them.

Finally, the history of technology shows a pattern: new tools do not eliminate professionals; they **raise the standard** of what professionals can do. Calculators did not replace accountants. CAD did not replace architects. AI will make software engineers faster and more productive, but it will also increase demand for people who can design, review, integrate, and govern complex systems.

So the real future is not replacement, but augmentation. AI will change software engineering—but it will not replace the software engineer, because the job is ultimately about human judgment, accountability, and problem-solving in the real world.
